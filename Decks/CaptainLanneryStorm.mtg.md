This deck plays like an aggressive pirate pile with a strong snowball plan. The low curve gets bodies onto the battlefield early, raid cards reward attacking, and Treasure from Captain Lannery Storm helps bridge into the larger threats.

Once the board is established, the list gets much harder to race. Dire Fleet Neckbreaker turns pirate attacks into real damage, Captivating Crew steals opposing threats, and the Treasure package lets Captain Lannery Storm keep growing while the deck keeps advancing its board.

## Game Plan

The ideal opening is simple: deploy cheap pirates, attack as soon as possible, and make raid live. Grasping Scoundrel, Fanatical Firebrand, Rigging Runner, and Shock keep the first turns active, while Treasure makers and value pirates add extra resources as the game develops.

From there, the deck wants to turn combat into a leverage engine. Captain Lannery Storm produces Treasure and threatens explosive turns, Dire Fleet Neckbreaker makes every attack hit harder, and Captivating Crew can convert an opponent's best creature into a finishing tool for your side.

<details>
<summary>Detailed Strategy</summary>
<br>

The deck is strongest when it keeps attacking without stalling. Raid creatures become much better once the board is already swinging, so the early turns should focus on getting any body into combat rather than holding back for value.

Treasure is the glue that keeps the list together. Captain Lannery Storm turns attacks into mana, which helps cast and activate the higher-end pirates, and that extra mana also makes it easier to keep pressure up after trading creatures.

Dire Fleet Neckbreaker is the cleanest payoff in the list because it rewards the exact thing the deck wants to do anyway: attack with pirates. Captivating Crew adds a different angle by stealing blockers or finishers, which makes racing awkward for any opponent trying to stabilize behind a single large creature.

Against decks with strong blockers, the best line is often to use Shock and attack triggers to force through small chunks of damage until Neckbreaker or Crew can create a decisive swing. Against slower decks, it is usually better to commit early and force them to answer multiple threats before Treasure starts snowballing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Additional mana details</summary>
<br>

The curve is heavily front-loaded, which fits a deck that wants to attack every turn and keep raid enabled. Most of the one-mana cards are there to start pressure or keep the board clear, while the four-mana slot contains the main payoff creatures.

The color base is straightforward Rakdos with four Blightstep Pathway as the flexible source. Because the list is so red-heavy, it generally wants red mana first and black mana second, especially when sequencing early threats into follow-up payoffs.

The color counts overlap because multicolor Pirates contribute to both red and black totals.

</details>

## Thoughts

The deck has a clean tribal identity and a strong combat loop, but it could be tuned further by tightening the highest-end slots around the cards that matter most in creature races. If the goal is speed, a few more one- and two-drop Pirates would make the early pressure more consistent.

If the goal is to maximize midgame power, the best upgrades are more cards that either create Treasure or make attacks more punishing. That keeps the deck aligned with Captain Lannery Storm's strengths instead of stretching it into a slower midrange shell.
