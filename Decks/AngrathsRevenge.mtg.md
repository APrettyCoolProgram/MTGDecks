## Overview

<div align="center">

| Deck |  |
|:--|:--|
| Archetype | Rakdos Angrath Midrange |
| Colors | ⚫🔴 |
| Strategy | Use efficient removal and discard effects to keep the board clear, then pressure life totals with aggressive Minotaurs and Angrath payoffs |
| Win Condition(s) | Combat damage, repeated burn and discard, Angrath's loyalty abilities, and Moraug or Treachery turns that steal tempo |
| Legalities | Not evaluated |

</div>

The deck is built to feel like an Angrath showcase rather than a pure curve deck. It mixes direct damage, sacrifice effects, and creature pressure so the game keeps moving in your favor even when attacks are stalled.

Minotaurs provide most of the body count, while the Angrath cards add the grindy angle. That gives the list a clear plan against creature decks: remove the best blocker, force damage through, and keep the opponent from stabilizing behind a single permanent.

## Game Plan

The first job is to stop the opponent from building a clean board. Cheap interaction like Shock, Sorin's Thirst, Murder, and Jaya's Greeting keeps creatures off the table, while Angrath's Rampage can break up whatever permanent matters most.

Once the game is slowed down, the deck wants to win through repeated pressure and awkward attacks. Minotaurs keep the damage coming, Angrath, Captain of Chaos makes combat harder to block, and Angrath, the Flame-Chained plus Moraug provide the kind of late-game swing that can end a stalled board in one turn.

<details>
<summary>Detailed Strategy</summary>
<br>

The deck plays best when it uses its removal proactively. Trading a small amount of mana for an early blocker often opens the door for a much better attack on the next turn, which is especially important when the curve starts to climb into four- and six-mana threats.

Angrath, Captain of Chaos is the cleanest bridge between the early and late game. Menace makes the whole team harder to stop, and the amass mode gives the deck a wider board without needing to commit more cards.

Angrath, the Flame-Chained and Angrath's Fury both reward a board that is already under control. The planeswalker pushes discard and stolen-creature play patterns, while the sorcery can remove a blocker and put the named Angrath card back into your hand if needed.

The modal lands help keep the mana base flexible, but they also make the list feel slightly more midrange than a pure aggro deck. When possible, use them to smooth the first few turns so the deck can keep casting spells instead of stumbling on color requirements.

</details>

<summary>Additional mana details</summary>

The curve starts low enough to interact early, but the deck is happiest in the midgame where its removal and creature threats line up together. The six-mana cards are the top end, so the list wants to survive the first few turns and then use mana efficiency to pull ahead.

The mana base is built to support red first and black second. The modal and utility lands smooth awkward draws, and the nonbasic package gives the deck enough flexibility to cast its mix of red burn, black removal, and double-angled Angrath cards.

Color counts overlap because several cards are multicolor.

</details>

## Thoughts

The deck already has a coherent tribal core, so the cleanest upgrades would keep the Minotaur shell intact while trimming the weakest standalone spells. If you want the list to feel faster, lower the number of six-mana cards and add more one- and two-drop interaction.

If you want it to feel more like an Angrath control deck, add more sacrifice, discard, or recursive threat cards that make the planeswalkers matter longer. That pushes the list toward grindy games where the emblematic red-black commander style gets to shine.
